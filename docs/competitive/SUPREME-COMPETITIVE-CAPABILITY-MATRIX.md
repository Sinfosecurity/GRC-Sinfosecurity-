## 1. Executive summary

Supreme is a **unified governance platform** (TPRM + Risk + Compliance + Privacy + AI + Automation + Graph + Insurance-as-configuration) with unusually strict honesty rules. #12 is **PASS** for **private-testing release-candidate** scope only. That is not a commercial product.

Public competitor evidence shows mature suites are stronger on:

- licensed regulatory content
- integration ecosystems
- external cyber ratings and vendor exchanges
- live SSO/SCIM proof
- no-code workflow
- AI assessment autofill
- market-recognized implementation partners

Supreme’s **defensible** (not unique) strengths are: Third Party ≠ Engagement with hosted multiple-engagement proof; requester / GRC / vendor isolation; Question ≠ Finding and Signal ≠ Finding; fail-closed malware on Shared Evidence; human-authoritative residual risk; Insurance as edition configuration rather than a fork; Governance Graph; refusal to invent compliance / exposure percentages.

Third Party ≠ Engagement is **not unique**. OneTrust, ServiceNow, Archer, MetricStream, ProcessUnity, and Aravo document engagement- or entity-level objects. It **is** a real differentiator versus Whistic, SecurityScorecard, BitSight, AuditBoard/Optro, LogicGate, Vanta, and Drata, where public pages stay vendor-centric.

The sale-stopping gaps for a large enterprise buyer today are commercial and operational, not missing TPRM theory: no commercial GO / live Stripe, live IdP not proven, live integrations not proven, no SOC 2 / ISO trust program, thin licensed content, no ratings fabric, no vendor-assessment exchange.

## 7. Domain comparisons

### TPRM

Supreme #12 private-testing RC has the operating path that enterprise TPRM RFPs ask for: intake → assignment → Third Party match → Engagement → IRA → human tier → plan → vendor questionnaire → evidence → finding (if warranted) → effectiveness → residual → treatment/acceptance → contract gate → activate → monitor → reassess → offboard.

Competitors that also document a **Third Party vs Engagement** (or entity vs engagement) model: OneTrust, ServiceNow, Archer, MetricStream, ProcessUnity, Aravo. Supreme’s hosted Microsoft 365 vs Azure proof is real. It is **not** a unique invention.

Supreme is behind on questionnaire **content** (SIG / CAIQ / CSA), vendor **networks**, **ratings adapters**, and **live integrations**. ProcessUnity Global Risk Exchange (vendor-claimed 18,000+ attestations / 370,000+ profiles) and Whistic Trust Center Exchange are category features Supreme does not have.

### GRC / workflow

LogicGate and MetricStream publicly sell **no-code / low-code** application building. ServiceNow sells the Now Platform. Supreme Automation (#20) is a governed template/scan engine, not a customer application studio. Do not claim workflow-builder parity.

### Risk

#15 is an accepted enterprise register with appetite, treatment, and acceptance that **does not rewrite residual**. Archer Insight / LogicGate Quantify sell **quantitative / FAIR** risk. Supreme does not. That is a specialized gap, not a #23 gap.

### Compliance

#16 is honest readiness, not “certified.” Archer (vendor-claimed 7,000+ / 22M+ sources), AuditBoard RegComply (vendor-claimed 10,000+ issuing bodies), LogicGate + Ascent, and OneTrust (50+ frameworks) are **content-library** products. Supreme has reference packs. Licensed global content belongs to **#27–#31**, not a new #23 phase.

### Privacy

OneTrust and ServiceNow Privacy Management are the documented privacy-ops peers. MetricStream, AuditBoard, LogicGate, ProcessUnity, Whistic, SSC, and BitSight did **not** show a dedicated privacy-ops product on pages reviewed. Supreme #17 is a real module. It is not OneTrust Consent / DSR-at-scale.

### AI Governance

OneTrust AI Governance, ServiceNow AI Control Tower, and LogicGate AI Governance are documented standalone products with EU AI Act / NIST / ISO 42001 content and, in OneTrust’s case, runtime guardrails. Supreme #18 is an accepted inventory + human-decision product. It does not have runtime guardrails or an MCP governance SDK. Do not claim AI-platform parity.

### Automation

Most competitors document automation. Supreme’s constraint is honesty: automation may not silently change residual, applicability, or approve AI. That is a product rule, not a missing engine.

### Intelligence

#19 refuses simulated threat feeds. Competitors that sell ratings (SSC, BitSight, ProcessUnity Risk Index, OneTrust Risk Exchange) will look “smarter” in demos unless Supreme is explicit that Intelligence is **recorded-data attention**, not outside-in cyber.

### Identity / API

#21 and #22 are the **nearest existing roadmap gaps** already named on 2026-09-18. Architecture exists. Live Entra/Okta/Google and live Slack/Jira are **NOT TESTED**. Enterprise RFPs will treat that as not done.

### Insurance

See section 12. Supreme must not claim policy administration, quoting, claims processing, or treaty admin. Archer RMIS is the only reviewed GRC-family product that documents insurance policies/claims/incidents as a product. ProcessUnity has a P&C insurer **case study**, not an insurance edition SKU.
